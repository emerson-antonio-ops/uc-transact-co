The four tables

“Just read the four tables” — four different problems

Each one breaks a different assumption — read the bottom line of every card.

01
public.customers
“Migrated from the legacy store in 2021.”
customer_id BIGINT PK signup_date DATE created_at TIMESTAMPTZ ingested_at TIMESTAMPTZ
Breaks: that the data starts where the table starts. You inherited a migration you cannot see.
02
public.products
“Prices are current, not historical.”
sku TEXT price NUMERIC(12,2) cost NUMERIC(12,2) — no valid_from / valid_to
Breaks: joining to price a past order. Only orders.unit_price knows what was actually charged.
03
public.orders
“One row per order line. Denormalized on purpose.”
order_id BIGINT PK product_id BIGINT quantity INTEGER total_amount NUMERIC(12,2)
Breaks: counting orders by counting rows. An order with three products is three rows.
04
public.payments
“Payment attempts and captures from the PSP webhook.”
payment_id BIGINT PK order_id BIGINT amount NUMERIC(12,2) status TEXT
Breaks: one order, one payment. A failed attempt is still a row — and still has an amount.
and one fact that governs all four — from the schema header
No foreign keys. No check constraints. Deliberately. An orphan payment, a negative price, an order pointing at a customer who does not exist — none of those can be written into a database that enforces integrity. This one accepts them all, in silence.

The anatomy

A prompt is a contract for the next piece of work

Eight rows. Each one closes a gap you just watched the agent fill on its own.

Why ontology

Ontology preserves meaning while systems change

make land copies every column perfectly. Not one of the decisions travels with it.

payment_id BIGINT order_id BIGINT amount NUMERIC(12,2) status TEXT paid_at TIMESTAMPTZ
make land · 62 000 rows · 0 errors
payment_id BIGINT order_id BIGINT amount DECIMAL(12,2) status VARCHAR paid_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE

Two engines, two type vocabularies, identical values. The crossing is lossless in every way a computer can measure — and that is exactly the problem.

What the copy left behind
01 status arrived as text. Which of its values count as revenue did not.
02 paid_at arrived as a timestamp. Whether payment is the recognition event did not.
03 A refunded row arrived intact. Whether it subtracts, and on which date, did not.
04 The UTC offset arrived. Whose calendar defines “yesterday” did not.
05 Payment and order amounts still reconcile. Nothing declares that they must.
06 No column carries an owner. Nobody in either database can decide any of the above.
the concept, stated once
Meaning is not a data type, so no migration can carry it. An ontology is the artifact that holds meaning outside both engines — entities, relationships, rules, events, provenance and owners, written down so they survive the crossing, the refactor, and the next warehouse.
The canonical ontology

One canonical layer between the schema and the answer

Every statement carries a status. The unresolved one is why the agent may refuse.

Ontology v1

Four solid edges — and one that refuses to close

The last edge is conditional on purpose. The data for it exists; the business contract does not.

subjectpredicateobjectstatusevidence
CustomerplacesOrder inferred orders.customer_id = customers.customer_id
OrdercontainsProduct inferred orders.product_id = products.product_id

Paymentmay_contribute_toRevenue unresolved candidate data exists · the business contract does not
read the third predicate again
It says has_payment_record, not settled by. A payment row proves a physical association — nothing more. Settlement and revenue recognition are business events, and the schema has never seen one. The weaker word is the honest word.
The distinction that matters

A graph is not a semantic layer

The graph says how things connect. It does not say how the metric is calculated, or who signed off.

“Knowledge graphs are strong at context. Semantic layers are strong at trusted execution.

Without a semantic layer, an AI agent may understand relationships but calculate the wrong metric.

Without a knowledge graph, the agent may calculate the metric correctly but miss important surrounding context. Production AI for regulated workloads needs both.”

Nilesh Kumar · Colrows ◆ Semantic Layer vs. Knowledge Graph, 21 Jun 2026
this lands directly on our case
Our graph already knows that a payment belongs to an order, and an order to a customer. It still cannot tell you whether R$ 1.284.903,55 is yesterday's revenue — because the statuses, the recognition event, the refund rule and the timezone were never decided. That is the CFO's question from slide one, restated as an architecture problem.
